Keep: who the user is, as an agent working with them would want to know it: their role and the field they work in, what they know well and what they are new to, the languages they read and write, the systems and machines they work on and how those are set up, where their tools are.
Leave out: names, e-mail addresses, account names and other identifiers; their company's or team's ways, the services they use and the work they are doing, which are memory beyond one project ("general"); how agents should work with them, which is a rule; what holds only today; personal matters that have nothing to do with the work.
Write it as a fact about the user, in the present tense.
Good: "Develops on Windows with WSL (Ubuntu); knows Go well and is new to Rust."
Not this: "Commits as Alex <alex@example.com>." (an identifier)
